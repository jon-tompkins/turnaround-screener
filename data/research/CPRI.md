# CPRI — Capri Holdings Limited

**Research note · 2026-09-18 · post-Versace deleveraged consumer turnaround**

Price $14.85 · Mkt cap $1.70B · EV $2.98B · Net debt ~$222M (FY26 year-end)
Shares 114.8M · Float 110.6M · Short 10.3% of float · Fwd P/E 5.9 · P/B 12.2
52-week range $12.40 – $28.27 · Analyst mean target $22.53 (16 analysts; high $45.5, low $16)

---

## The setup in one paragraph

Capri is what's left of a luxury conglomerate after the deal machine broke. The
$8.5B Tapestry merger was blocked by the FTC in late 2024, and rather than
limp on as a three-brand group, management sold **Versace to Prada for $1.375B
in cash (closed Dec 2, 2025)** and pivoted the entire company to fixing its two
remaining brands: **Michael Kors (~83% of revenue) and Jimmy Choo (~17%)**. The
Versace cash took **net debt from ~$1.4B to ~$222M**, restarted buybacks ($79M
in Q4 FY26, $200M planned in FY27 against a $1B authorization), and gave Capri
a clean, deleveraged balance sheet for the first time in years. The stock still
trades near multi-year lows at **5.9x forward earnings** with the CEO and the
entire board buying stock in the open market. This is an early, "basing"
turnaround: the balance sheet is fixed and EPS is set to grow ~40% in FY27, but
the top line is still shrinking and the Michael Kors brand turn is unproven.

This is the highest-conviction name in today's screener output (conviction 7),
but conviction here rests on capital structure and insider signal, not yet on a
confirmed operating inflection.

---

## Why this is interesting

### 1. The balance sheet went from a liability to a non-issue

Pre-Versace, Capri carried ~$1.8B of debt against a shrinking, discretionary
business — the kind of leverage that turns a cyclical soft patch into an
existential one. The $1.375B Versace sale changed the risk profile entirely:
**net debt is ~$222M**, and management has explicitly prioritized deleveraging
plus buybacks over M&A. At an EV of ~$3.0B, the debt is no longer the story.
This removes the tail risk that usually kills value traps in discretionary
retail.

### 2. Insiders are buying with real money, broadly

This is the standout signal. Over the trailing six months insiders were **net
buyers of ~1.63M shares across 13 transactions** — with sales of just ~92k.
Specifically:
- **CEO John Idol bought 55,000 shares (~$989k) at ~$17.98 on March 11, 2026**,
  lifting his direct stake ~2.5% to ~2.26M shares.
- The **entire board** participated in open-market buys (the July 29 tranche
  shows six directors each adding).

At today's $14.85 the stock trades **below Idol's ~$18 buy price** — you're
getting in cheaper than the CEO did. Broad, coordinated insider buying near the
lows is one of the more reliable turnaround tells.

### 3. EPS is engineered to grow ~40% even with a flat/soft top line

Management maintained **FY27 adjusted EPS guidance of ~$2.15 (≈40% growth over
FY26)** even after trimming the FY27 revenue outlook to ~$3.4B. The growth comes
from three levers that don't require a revenue boom:
- **Margin recovery** — FY27 adjusted operating income targeted at ~$170M, with
  Michael Kors margin moving to low-double-digits and Jimmy Choo to low-single.
- **Interest expense collapse** from deleveraging.
- **Share count reduction** — $200M of FY27 buybacks on a $1.7B cap is ~12% of
  shares if executed near current prices, a direct EPS tailwind.

At 5.9x forward earnings, the market is paying almost nothing for the guided
growth to materialize.

### 4. Jimmy Choo is quietly working

The small brand is the bright spot: **Q1 FY27 revenue +10.5%**, with adjusted
operating margin at **7.3%, up ~480bps YoY**. It's only 17% of the mix, but it
proves the operating playbook (product refresh, pricing, marketing) can lift a
Capri brand's profitability — the template management now has to run on the much
larger Michael Kors.

### 5. Recent quarters beat depressed expectations

The last two reported quarters delivered EPS surprises of **+67% and +83%**.
That pattern usually means the sell-side cut numbers too aggressively into the
Versace overhang, leaving a low bar the company keeps clearing.

---

## Why this could fail

### 1. Michael Kors is still shrinking, and it's 83% of the company

The core brand fell ~5.6% in the last reported quarter, and Q1 FY27 op margin
actually slipped ~60bps YoY on expense deleverage. The whole thesis rests on MK
comps inflecting from negative to flat/positive. Until that shows up in a print,
this is a story stock. **Aspirational/accessible-luxury brands that fall into
the discount-and-promotion spiral are notoriously hard to reheat** — Michael
Kors has been fighting brand fatigue for years.

### 2. The top line is still declining

Revenue fell to **$3.47B (FY26) from $4.14B (FY24)**, and FY27 guidance was
**cut to ~$3.4B** on inventory delays, FX, and EMEA softness. "Basing" is the
right screen mode: the bottom may be forming, but it is not confirmed, and a
consumer slowdown would push the base lower.

### 3. Discretionary, cyclical, and consumer-dependent

Handbags and footwear are pure discretionary spend. A weakening US/EMEA consumer
or a luxury pullback hits Capri directly, and it has less brand pricing power
than true luxury houses (LVMH, Hermès). The turnaround needs a cooperative macro
it can't control.

### 4. Buyback is a use of cash, not a fix for demand

A $200M buyback flatters EPS but doesn't sell more handbags. If management is
repurchasing shares while the brand keeps eroding, it's financial engineering
masking an operating decline — exactly the trap value names fall into.

### 5. Thin equity base / optical leverage ratios

Reported debt-to-equity is very high (equity is small after years of writedowns)
and price-to-book is ~12x — so this is not "cheap on assets," only cheap on
forward earnings. If those earnings don't come, there's little tangible backstop.

## Probability-weighted EV math

Anchoring on the FY27 adj EPS guide (~$2.15) and a plausible FY28 path, valued
off a normalized multiple. Current price **$14.85**.

| Scenario | Prob | FY28 EPS | Multiple | Target | Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** — MK comps inflect positive, buyback executes, re-rates | 30% | $3.00 | 11x | $33 | +122% |
| **Base** — margins + buyback deliver ~$2.15→$2.60, modest re-rate | 45% | $2.50 | 8x | $20 | +35% |
| **Bear** — MK keeps eroding, guide cut again, stays cheap | 25% | $1.50 | 6x | $9 | −39% |

**Probability-weighted target ≈ $21.4 → ~+44% expected return.** The mean
sell-side target ($22.53) sits right in that base/bull zone, and the two-year
bull case ($33–$38) aligns with at least one published analyst framework citing
reduced net debt, positive FCF, the $200M buyback, and the FY27 guide.

Asymmetry is favorable but not extreme: ~+44% weighted upside against a ~40%
drawdown in the bear leg. The insider buying and fixed balance sheet are what
tilt the odds toward the base/bull outcomes.

## Position sizing

**2–4% of a speculative turnaround sleeve.** Enough to matter if MK inflects,
small enough to survive the bear leg. This is a "basing" name without a hard
single-date catalyst, so size for patience, not for an event pop. Scale in
rather than taking a full position at once — you want dry powder if a soft
quarter prints a cheaper entry.

---

## Entry plan

- **Accumulate $13.00–$15.00.** Today's $14.85 is inside the zone and below the
  CEO's ~$18 cost basis.
- **Add on weakness toward the $12.40 52-week low** only if the balance sheet
  and buyback remain intact (i.e., price weakness without thesis breakage).
- Avoid chasing above ~$17 pre-inflection; wait for a positive MK comp print to
  justify paying up.

## Exit plan

- **First trim at $20–$22** (analyst mean, base-case target) — take partial
  profits, this is where the easy re-rating from "fixed balance sheet" is done.
- **Core target $33–$38** (two-year bull) — requires at least one quarter of
  positive Michael Kors comps and continued buyback execution.
- **Behavioral / thesis-break stops:**
  - Michael Kors comps deteriorate further (worse than -6%) for two straight
    quarters → exit; the turnaround isn't turning.
  - Net debt rises materially or the buyback is paused → exit; management is
    signaling the cash isn't there.
  - Price breaks and holds below ~$11 (under the 52-week low) → respect the
    tape, thesis is not working.

## Hedges

For a larger-than-4% position, the clean hedge is a broad discretionary/retail
short or long puts on a strong-consumer peer to isolate the CPRI-specific
turnaround from the macro consumer bet. Options IV is elevated (~68%), so buying
CPRI puts as a hedge is expensive; a peer/sector hedge is more cost-effective.
For most sleeves, correct sizing is the hedge.

---

## Specific watch items

- **Q2 FY26/FY27 earnings — Nov 3, 2026.** The single most important print:
  Michael Kors comp trajectory, MK operating margin (watch for the low-double-
  digit target), and any change to the $2.15 EPS guide.
- **Buyback pace** — confirm the $200M FY27 repurchase is actually executing
  quarter by quarter (share count decline).
- **Net debt trend** — should stay near/below ~$222M; any climb is a red flag.
- **Jimmy Choo momentum** — continued double-digit revenue growth validates the
  operating playbook that MK needs to replicate.
- **Inventory / EMEA commentary** — the reasons cited for the FY27 revenue cut;
  watch whether they resolve or worsen.
- **Further insider activity** — more buying near current levels reinforces the
  thesis; any cluster of sales would undercut it.

---

## One-sentence call

A genuinely de-risked balance sheet, a ~40% EPS-growth guide, a 12%-of-cap
buyback, and coordinated insider buying below the CEO's own cost basis make CPRI
an attractive early-turnaround long at 5.9x forward earnings — buy a starter in
the $13–$15 zone and add on a confirmed Michael Kors comp inflection, sizing for
patience because the top line hasn't turned yet.
