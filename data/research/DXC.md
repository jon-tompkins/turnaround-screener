# DXC Technology (DXC) — Deep Dive
*Written: 2026-07-17 | Analyst: Bob (auto)*

## Thesis

DXC Technology is a $12.6B revenue IT outsourcing business trading at ~3x EV/EBITDA and a ~40% FCF yield on FY2027 guided free cash flow. The stock was crushed 19% in May 2026 when Q4 FY26 revenue missed despite an EPS beat, reflecting the market's core concern: DXC's revenue is still shrinking. The bull case is that a new CEO (Fernandez, 2023), aggressive restructuring, and a credible AI pivot via a multi-year Anthropic alliance can arrest revenue decline and re-rate the multiple. With Q1 FY27 earnings on July 30 (13 days), a book-to-bill >1.0, CEO open-market buying, and three consecutive EPS beats, the risk/reward for a small pre-earnings position is acceptable — but this is firmly a 5/10 setup, not a table-pounder.

---

## Why This Is Interesting

**1. Absurdly cheap on cash generation metrics**
DXC guided FY2027 FCF at ~$600M against a current market cap of $1.54B (~39% FCF yield). The FY2026 actual FCF came in at $713M. EV/EBITDA sits at 2.97x. Forward P/E at the midpoint of FY27 non-GAAP EPS guidance ($2.65) is 3.6x. You can argue about GAAP vs non-GAAP all day, but the cash is real — $713M in FY26 with only modest capex requirements.

**2. CEO put real money in at current price**
Raul Fernandez (CEO since 2023) purchased 28,050 shares at $8.90-$9.00 on May 11, 2026 — the same week as the post-earnings selloff. This was an open-market purchase, not a grant. That's $250K of personal conviction at essentially the current price. Insiders don't always know better, but they know *something*.

**3. Three consecutive EPS beats — and the pattern is intact**
Q1 FY26: +20.5%, Q2 FY26: +16.2%, Q3 FY26: +9.5%. Beat magnitude is shrinking but still positive. Zacks Earnings ESP for Q1 FY27 (July 30) is +9.52%, meaning analysts have revised estimates up recently. The $0.45 Q1 estimate is likely conservative given historical seasonality and FY27 guidance midpoint implying ~$0.66/quarter average.

**4. Near-term debt cliff resolved — buybacks incoming**
DXC redeemed €650M of 2026 notes and replaced with €650M of 2030 notes at 4.25%. They also retired the $300M September 2026 notes. The refinancing clears the immediate balance sheet risk and extends maturities. Management guided H1 FY27 plans to retire an additional $400M in bonds AND repurchase $250M in shares — buybacks at 3x forward P/E are highly accretive.

**5. Anthropic alliance announced June 11, 2026 — AI narrative now in play**
DXC is one of few Global Premier partners in the Claude Partner Network. Claude is already powering DXC OASIS (their AI orchestration platform) in production with 50+ customers. DXC is certifying "tens of thousands" of engineers through Anthropic's Partner Academy in 90-day cohorts. This isn't vaporware — OASIS is already live and the 95%-Claude-generated code pipeline is operational. If DXC can win new business on AI modernization of legacy insurance/banking/government systems, revenue decline could stabilize.

## Why This Could Fail

**1. Revenue trajectory is the central problem — and it's guided to get worse**
FY27 guidance called for organic revenue decline of -3% to -5% (worse than the actual -1.2% in FY26). The market punished the stock 19% in one session because it sees the rate of revenue decline, not the margin management. Another quarter of missing revenue vs. consensus will trigger a second leg down regardless of EPS.

**2. GAAP vs non-GAAP gap is massive and suspicious**
Q4 FY26 GAAP EPS was -$0.84 while non-GAAP was +$0.77. The gap ($1.61/share!) consists of restructuring charges, amortization, and goodwill impairments. While non-GAAP FCF is real, the serial restructuring charges raise questions about how much of the margin improvement is permanent vs. extracted.

**3. Leverage is real even after refinancing**
Even with debt paydowns, DXC still carries ~$3B in long-term debt against a $1.54B market cap. Debt-to-equity at 132x. If FCF disappoints (guidance cut from $713M to $600M already), debt service pressure will constrain capital allocation flexibility and market multiple.

**4. Legacy IT outsourcing faces secular headwinds — Anthropic doesn't change the base**
The -5% to -3% revenue decline guidance reflects contract attrition as customers migrate workloads to cloud-native vendors (AWS, Azure, Google). DXC's legacy is mainframe management, ERP hosting, and IT infrastructure — categories under sustained pressure. The Anthropic alliance is additive but won't offset this in the near term.

**5. Thin analyst coverage = limited re-rating catalyst**
Only 7 analysts cover DXC. Mean target is $11.29. No major upgrade wave is coming to re-rate the multiple unless revenue stabilizes. This is a self-directed conviction trade, not a "wait for the Street to wake up" setup.
