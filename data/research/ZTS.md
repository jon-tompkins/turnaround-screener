## Thesis (one paragraph)

Zoetis is the global leader in animal health — the highest-quality business in today's screen batch (71.7% gross margin, 40.7% operating margin, 27.7% net margin) — that has been cut roughly in half from its 52-week high after two consecutive 2026 guidance reductions. The pain is concentrated in one place: the U.S. companion-animal business, where a multi-year decline in veterinary-clinic visits, pet-owner price sensitivity, generic erosion on older products (Cerenia, Convenia), and intensified competition in dermatology and parasiticides have knocked organic growth negative. The bull case is that this is a cyclical/affordability air-pocket in a structurally growing category, that the stock now trades at a trough multiple (~11–12x forward earnings versus a high-teens historical multiple and industry median ~17.5x), and that stabilization of Librela plus livestock strength set up a re-rating. The bear case is that declining vet visits are a durable behavioral shift and that Zoetis's premium innovation pricing model is structurally impaired. This is a quality-at-a-trough setup, not a distressed one — best owned as a patient starter position that scales on confirmation.

## Why this is interesting

1. **Best-in-class economics on sale.** Zoetis runs 71.7% gross / 40.7% operating / 27.7% net margins with a durable, diversified franchise across companion animal (Simparica, Librela/Solensia, Apoquel/Cytopoint dermatology) and livestock (cattle, poultry, fish). Businesses this good rarely trade near a multi-year low; the de-rate creates the opportunity.

2. **Valuation reset to a trough.** Forward P/E has compressed to roughly 11.6x on one measure (about 34% below the drug-manufacturer industry median of ~17.5x), versus the high-teens-to-20s multiple Zoetis carried for most of its post-IPO life. Even the more conservative ~19x reading on other estimate sets is well below its history. You are paying a below-market multiple for an above-market franchise.

3. **The weakness is narrow and identifiable.** The guide-down is almost entirely U.S. companion animal (sub-segment -11%, U.S. segment revenue -$90M / -7% in Q2 2026, companion-animal products -$132M). Livestock is actually strong — cattle demand rose on the New World screwworm response and better supply. A problem this concentrated is more fixable than a broad-based demand collapse.

4. **Librela stabilizing.** The OA-pain monoclonal antibody fell ~22% in Q1 2026 but management flagged U.S. stabilization and traction from corrective efforts by Q2. Librela remains a first-in-class, high-value franchise (US$65–175/month) with a large untreated canine-OA population; a return to growth would restore a key narrative pillar.

5. **Shareholder returns intact.** Dividend is well covered (payout ~33%, ~2.8% trailing yield) with ongoing buybacks. A cash-generative leader buying back stock at a trough multiple is accretive, and the dividend provides a paid-to-wait floor.

## Why this could fail

1. **Declining vet visits may be structural, not cyclical.** Management itself calls it "a multiyear trend of declining veterinary clinic visits." If pet-owner behavior has permanently reset lower (fewer visits, trading down to generics/OTC), Zoetis's premium-innovation model loses its growth engine and the multiple deserves to stay compressed.

2. **Competition and generics are real.** Dermatology and Simparica Trio face heightened competitive intensity; Cerenia and Convenia are being eroded by generics. This is margin- and share-eroding, and it is happening in the highest-value franchises.

3. **Earnings are still declining, not just decelerating.** 2026 guidance is organic revenue -3% to -1% and adjusted net income -9% to -5%. The screener flags this as "basing," but the fundamental trend is still down; catching it before the trough is confirmed risks another leg lower.

4. **Two cuts already — a third would break the "trough" thesis.** ZTS dropped ~21–25% on the Q1 guide-down and cut again with Q2. Guidance credibility is impaired; a third consecutive reduction would signal the affordability/visit problem is deeper than framed and could send the stock through the $71 low.

5. **Librela safety scrutiny.** The FDA CVM adverse-event database carries thousands of Librela reports (neurological signs; RPOA discussion in the literature). A regulatory label change or intensified vet caution could re-pressure the franchise just as it is stabilizing.
