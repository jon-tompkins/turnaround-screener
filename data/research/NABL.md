# N-able, Inc. (NABL) — Deep Dive
**Date:** 2026-07-07 | **Price:** $4.395 | **Market cap:** ~$828M | **Screen mode:** basing | **Conviction:** 6/10

## Thesis

N-able is the only pure-play public company in the RMM/MSP (remote monitoring & management / managed service provider) software space, trading at a steep discount to both broader software multiples and to the private-market valuations of its top competitor. It just delivered a genuine beat-and-raise Q1 — 13.1% ttm revenue growth, reaffirmed FY26 guidance, active buybacks — yet the stock is down ~51% from its 52-week high and kept drifting lower through Q2. The sell-off looks driven by sector-wide growth-deceleration and margin-normalization anxiety plus skepticism about a "back-half weighted" FY26 plan, not by company-specific deterioration. The setup is a real, profitable, growing business priced like a broken one, with the August 6 earnings print as the near-term test of whether the H2 ramp materializes.

## Why This Is Interesting

1. **Cheapest way to buy the RMM/MSP software category.** NABL trades at forward EV/Sales of ~1.6x and forward EV/EBITDA of ~5.2x against sector medians of ~3.7x and ~14.9x respectively. Its closest competitor by growth and funding, NinjaOne, was valued at $12.3B in a June 2026 private round on >$600M ARR — roughly 20x ARR, versus NABL's public multiple that implies a fraction of that per dollar of ARR. The market is pricing NABL as a legacy laggard even though it holds the #2 global MSP software market share (~16.2%) behind ConnectWise.

2. **The Q1 print was a real beat, not a low-quality one.** Revenue of $133.7M beat the $131.6M estimate, EPS of $0.09 beat the $0.08 estimate, and management explicitly credited MDR cross-sell into the existing base for net revenue retention gains — a sign the growth is coming from monetizing the installed base, not just headline bookings.

3. **New product cycle gives a real reason for growth to reaccelerate, not just "hope."** AI-powered XDR via the Adlumin acquisition, a new Disaster-Recovery-as-a-Service offering, and a new Google backup product are all real, shipping SKUs — not vague roadmap promises. Management flagged all three as "back-half weighted," which is a real execution risk (see below) but also means the Q1 numbers already understate what the business could do if the ramp lands.

4. **Capital return signals management thinks the stock is cheap.** The company repurchased 1.29M shares for ~$10M in the latest tranche (~$29.9M cumulative under the March 2025 authorization) — buying back stock at a price roughly half of where it traded a year ago.

5. **Sponsor (Silver Lake) still has skin in the game, no exit signal.** Silver Lake-affiliated directors received fresh RSU grants on June 2, 2026, and 13F tracking still shows Silver Lake as a portfolio holder. No secondary offering or block sale was found. The one insider sale on record (CEO Pagliuca, 21,323 shares on May 15 at $3.32) was disclosed as a tax-withholding disposition tied to RSU vesting, not a discretionary open-market sale — a meaningfully different signal than a founder cashing out.

## Why This Could Fail

1. **The guide is explicitly back-half weighted, and the new SKUs are unproven at scale.** DRaaS and the Google backup product are new; if they don't ramp on the assumed timeline, N-able would need to make up the shortfall in H2 with no margin for error, and FY26 guidance (already reaffirmed once) could get cut at the Q2 print — the same pattern that hit INSP earlier this cycle.

2. **Leverage is real, unlike a typical cash-rich software story.** Debt/equity of ~54.5% and total debt of $436M against an $828M market cap leaves less room for error than a typical high-margin SaaS balance sheet. If growth stalls, deleveraging optionality narrows fast.

3. **NinjaOne is a well-funded, faster-growing private threat.** A $12.3B private valuation gives NinjaOne enormous capital to spend on customer acquisition and undercut on price — it is explicitly the "main share-pressure risk" identified in competitive research, and it doesn't face public-market quarterly scrutiny the way N-able does.

4. **Kaseya (post-Datto) is the scale leader in the category.** Kaseya absorbed Datto in 2022 and is now, combined with ConnectWise, controlling the largest share of a market where the top five vendors already hold two-thirds of global revenue. N-able is fighting for share against a consolidated #1 (Kaseya/Datto) and a well-capitalized insurgent (NinjaOne) simultaneously.

5. **Analyst sentiment is genuinely split, not just noisy.** Needham cut its target to $6.50 from $8 (while keeping Buy) even after the beat-and-raise quarter, citing execution risk on the back-half plan. Scotiabank raised its target modestly. This isn't unanimous skepticism, but it isn't a consensus re-rating catalyst either — the stock needs to prove it, not get re-rated on sentiment alone.

## Probability-Weighted EV Math

| Scenario | Probability | Price Target | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | 25% | $7.50 | Q2 print confirms H2 SKU ramp on track, ARR growth trends toward the high end of 8-9% guide, multiple re-rates toward 2.5-3x EV/Sales as the "cheapest public MSP name" narrative gains traction |
| **Base** | 45% | $5.25 | Guidance reaffirmed again with modest execution noise, stock drifts back toward analyst mean target ($6.85) is not achieved but downside stabilizes as buybacks continue |
| **Bear** | 30% | $3.00 | Back-half SKU ramp disappoints, guidance gets trimmed, leverage becomes a talking point, stock re-tests or breaks the 52-week low ($2.92) |

Expected value: (0.25 × $7.50) + (0.45 × $5.25) + (0.30 × $3.00) = $1.875 + $2.3625 + $0.90 = **$5.14**, roughly 17% above the current $4.395 price — a positive but not overwhelming EV skew, consistent with a 6/10 (not higher) conviction score.

## Position Sizing

**2-3% of portfolio as a starter position.** This is not a high-conviction binary catalyst trade — it's a valuation-gap thesis that needs the August earnings print to start validating. Given the real leverage on the balance sheet and the back-half-weighted guidance risk, sizing should stay modest until the Q2 print either confirms the ramp (add) or flags execution slippage (cut).

## Entry Plan

- **Initial tranche:** current levels, ~$4.30-$4.45
- **Add tranche:** on a post-earnings pullback into the $3.75-$4.00 range if the print is merely in-line (not a miss) — the stock already drifted into this range as of July 1, so this level has recent support
- Avoid chasing above $5.00 pre-earnings; wait for confirmation

## Exit Plan

**On success:** trim a third of the position into any move to $6.50-$7.00 (the low end of the bull scenario and near the Needham-cut target), let the remainder run toward the $8.50-$9.00 area (near 52-week high) only if H2 SKU ramp is visibly working by Q3.

**On failure:** if the Aug 6 print shows ARR growth decelerating below the 8% guided floor, or FY26 guidance gets cut, exit in full — don't average down into a back-half-weighted guide that's already missing. A break of the $3.60-$3.75 support zone (recent trading range) on high volume post-earnings is the hard stop.

## Hedges

Given the 2-3% position size, no hedge is necessary at this sizing. If the position is later scaled up post-earnings confirmation (toward 5%+), consider a small NABL put spread (e.g., $4/$3 puts, ~6-8 weeks out) financed by trimming upside calls, to cap tail risk from a guidance cut given the balance sheet leverage.

## Specific Watch Items / Hard Catalyst Dates

- **August 6, 2026 — Q2 earnings.** The single most important date: confirms or disconfirms whether H1 tracked toward the back-half-loaded FY26 plan.
- **DRaaS and Google backup product traction** — watch for management commentary on attach rates / early bookings for these new SKUs on the Q2 call.
- **Adlumin/XDR cross-sell metrics** — net revenue retention trend is the tell for whether the MDR cross-sell story (credited for the Q1 beat) is continuing or was a one-quarter pull-forward.
- **Bengaluru Global Capability Centre (opened June 15, 2026)** — a cost lever relevant to the 30-31% EBITDA margin guide; watch for margin commentary tied to this on the Q2 call.
- **Further Silver Lake Form 4 activity** — any shift from RSU grants to open-market selling would be a signal worth re-underwriting the thesis around.
- **Buyback pace** — continued repurchases at current depressed levels would reinforce the "management thinks it's cheap" signal; a pause would be a mild negative tell.

## One-Sentence Call

Small starter position (2-3%) into the August 6 earnings print — N-able is a genuinely growing, profitable, cheap software business trading like a broken one, but the back-half-weighted guide and real balance-sheet leverage mean this is a "prove it" trade, not a high-conviction one yet.
