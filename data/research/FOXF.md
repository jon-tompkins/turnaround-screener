# FOXF — Fox Factory Holding Corp.

**Research note · 2026-08-05 · recovering turnaround with a hard near-term catalyst**

Price ~$19.64 · Mkt cap ~$824M · Net debt ~$634M · EV ~$1.56B
FY2025 (Jan'26) revenue $1.47B · Adj EBITDA ~$169M (~11% margin) · FY2025 GAAP NI -$544.6M (impairment-driven)
FY2026 guide: revenue $1.33-1.42B · Adj EBITDA $174-203M (~200bps margin expansion to ~13%)

---

## Thesis in one paragraph

Fox Factory is a premium enthusiast-product company (ride-control suspension for
off-road trucks, powersports, bikes, plus Marucci baseball/softball) that traded
at $30+ before a brutal FY2025: a ~$545M GAAP net loss driven by impairment
charges, margin compression from tariffs and mix, and a leveraged balance sheet
inherited from the 2022 acquisition spree. New CEO Mike Dennison (installed
early 2025) launched a two-phase **$50M profit optimization plan** ($10M phase-1
carryover + $40M phase-2 net new) and a **portfolio tightening** program that
sold the Phoenix, AZ UTV operations in Q1 2026 and is exploring strategic
alternatives for non-core assets — all proceeds earmarked for debt reduction.
Q1 2026 delivered: revenue at the high end, adj EBITDA above the high end, the
Phoenix divestiture closed on schedule, and full-year guidance reaffirmed with
200bps margin expansion weighted to H2. The stock is recovering off the $13 low
to ~$19.64, the multiple is reasonable (EV/EBITDA 10.6x, P/B 1.25x, forward PE
~14x on FY2026E EPS $1.40), and **Q2 2026 earnings land tomorrow (Aug 6)** — the
binary event that either confirms the cost-takeout story and opens the path to
$24+ or re-breaks the stock below the 200-day MA.

## Why this is interesting

### 1. The cost-takeout story is showing early proof

Phase 1 savings (~$10M) are flowing through, phase 2 (~$40M) is "on schedule" per
management, and Q1 2026 captured mid-single-digit millions of the $50M target —
tracking, not missing. Q1 adjusted EBITDA of $35.7M came in **above the high end
of guidance** despite tariff pressure and mix headwinds. This is the critical
evidence point: the plan is not aspirational, it's landing. FY2026 guidance
calls for adjusted EBITDA of $174-203M (midpoint ~$188M, ~13% margin, +200bps
YoY) and EPS of $1.40 (+31.9% YoY), with Wall analysts modeling FY2027 EPS of
$1.74 (+24.7%) — a real earnings recovery trajectory rather than a one-quarter
beat.

### 2. De-levering with a credible path

Net debt slipped to ~$634M at Q1 2026 (from $616M at FY25 close), driven by
seasonal working capital — Q1 is seasonally the hardest quarter, with incentive
comp payouts and H1 tariff cash impacts. But management **proactively amended
the credit agreement** to expand covenant headroom (leverage ratio at quarter
end was ~3.77x, comfortably inside covenants) and remains committed to debt
reduction: continued EBITDA contribution from cost-out, divestiture proceeds
(Phoenix closed, more likely), and sub-2% capex intensity. The bond market signal
that an amendment was completed "from a position of strength" is important — this
isn't a distressed renegotiation.

### 3. Portfolio tightening creates optionality

The Phoenix UTV divestiture closed in Q1 as planned. Management is openly
exploring "strategic alternatives" for other non-core assets, though CEO
Dennison was careful to say they're **running Marucci hard** in the interim, not
shopping it actively. Each divestiture accomplishes two things: reduces leverage
and refocuses management bandwidth on the core ride-control and aftermarket
franchises (PVG + AAG) where FOX has genuine brand dominance. If the SSG
business (Marucci + bike) is eventually sold, FOXF simplifies into a cleaner
off-road/aftermarket play at a lower leverage profile — a re-rating catalyst.

### 4. Valuation is not pricing in the recovery

At ~$19.64, FOXF trades:
- **EV/EBITDA 10.6x** FY2026E (midpoint $188M) — below the ~13-18x range typical
  for branded consumer-discretionary companies with sustained earnings power
- **Forward PE ~14x** on FY2026E EPS $1.40, dropping to ~11x on FY2027E $1.74
- **P/B 1.25x** — close to book, a rare level for a brand franchise
- **EV/Revenue 1.06x** — a cigarette-butt multiple on a business with 30%+ gross
  margins and a premium brand portfolio

The analyst consensus is Hold with a $21.67 target (+9.4% upside), but there's
a wide dispersion: Stifel at $24 Buy (+21.2%), BofA at $20 Sell (+1.0%). The
dispersion itself is a signal — the setup is asymmetric if the H2 margin
expansion thesis plays out.

### 5. Short squeeze optionality

Short interest is **11.5% of float** with a short ratio of 5.3 days. A Q2 beat
that reaffirms H2 margin expansion (especially if Marucci bike supply and F-150
volume disruptions resolve as guide-to-nothing timing items) could force shorts
to cover into a thin float. This is not the core thesis — it's an asymmetric
optionality layered on top of a recovery that's already underway.

## Why this could fail

### 1. The earnings print is binary and tomorrow

Q2 2026 guidance is soft: revenue $343-365M (-7% YoY at midpoint) and Adj EBITDA
$32-40M (down from $35.7M in Q1). Management flagged three discrete drags: a
delayed Marucci product launch, a bike supplier disruption, and lower F-150
upfit volumes from an industry-wide aluminum supply issue. They insisted the
Marucci and bike items are **timing shifts to Q3**, and F-150 back-half volumes
are intact — but if any of these slip further, or if Q2 misses the already-soft
guide, the stock likely re-tests the $13-15 range. A binary event one day out
is a risk, not a feature.

### 2. Revenue is still declining

FY2026E revenue of $1.40B is **-4.5% YoY** — this is a cost story, not a growth
story. If end markets (powersports, truck aftermarket, bikes) deteriorate beyond
management's "not counting on a recovery" framework, the cost-takeout has to
work harder just to hold margins. Fox isn't a business that can grow its way out
of problems right now.

### 3. Leverage is still high

Net debt ~$634M against FY2026E adj EBITDA of ~$188M is **~3.4x leverage** at
the midpoint — not catastrophic, but at the high end for a cyclical consumer
discretionary name. If EBITDA disappoints, the leverage ratio climbs, the
amended covenant headroom shrinks, and the equity story flips from "deleveraging
turnaround" to "balance sheet problem." The 3.77x Q1 print is a reminder that
this is still a leveraged equity.

### 4. Marucci is a drag and a strategic question mark

Specialty Sports Group revenue was $498.6M TTM (largest segment by revenue),
with Marucci (baseball/softball) specifically called out as weak in Q1 due to
channel inventory overflow. The CEO's careful phrasing — "we are running that
business hard" rather than "we are committed to holding it" — leaves the door
open to a future divestiture, but until that's resolved, Marucci remains a
margin-dilutive overhang on the story.

### 5. Operating margin is thin with little room for error

Q1 2026 adjusted operating margin was ~9.7% (adj EBITDA), but GAAP operating
margin is still deeply negative on a TTM basis due to the FY2025 impairment.
Gross margin is compressed to 28.9% (from 30.9% prior year) by tariffs and mix.
Steel and aluminum costs are pressuring Q2. Any further commodity inflation or
tariff escalation erodes the thin cushion between the cost-takeout plan and the
margin guide.

### 6. No insider buying

Insider transactions in the last 6 months are exclusively stock-award grants to
directors and officers — no open-market purchases. Insiders hold only ~412k
shares (~1% of shares outstanding). The CEO received a 149,585-share grant in
March 2026, but no director has put personal capital to work at these depressed
levels. That's a yellow flag on internal conviction.

    that's a clear negative signal from those closest to the business.
