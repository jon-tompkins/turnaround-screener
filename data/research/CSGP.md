# CoStar Group (CSGP) — Deep Dive

_Analyst note, 2026-08-14. Price at screen: ~$32.64. 52-wk range $25.89–$91.89. Market cap ~$13.2B, EV ~$13.3B (near net-cash)._

## Thesis (one paragraph)

CoStar is a wide-moat commercial-real-estate data monopoly whose stock got cut ~64% from its $92 high because a multi-year, self-funded land grab in residential (Homes.com) buried the reported margins of an otherwise 78%-gross-margin, 18-23%-growth franchise. That overhang is now inflecting: Q2 2026 (reported Jul 28) showed residential turn adjusted-EBITDA-positive for the first time since the Homes.com launch, consolidated adjusted EBITDA more than doubled YoY to $184M, and management reaffirmed a record FY26 EBITDA guide of $780-820M. The CEO put $2.5M of his own money in at $29.89 on Aug 4. If commercial margins keep expanding and residential losses keep shrinking, the market re-rates a "data utility masquerading as a cash-burning tech company" back toward its franchise value. This is a quality compounder in a self-inflicted margin trough, not a broken business — the classic setup this screener exists to find.

## Why this is interesting

1. **The bear thesis just started breaking.** The single biggest knock on CoStar was that Homes.com is an infinite cash furnace. Q2 2026 killed that framing: residential revenue grew 33% to $444M and posted its **first-ever positive adjusted EBITDA (+$12M)** since the 2024 launch. Homes.com revenue +66%, agent subscribers more than doubled to 36,000+. The narrative pivot from "when does the burn stop" to "residential is now additive" is exactly what re-rates a stock like this.

2. **Commercial franchise is a genuine moat, still compounding.** 78% gross margin (5pts above Zillow, 9pts above Verisk), 60+ consecutive quarters of revenue growth, and CRE adjusted-EBITDA margin already back to ~34%. The core CoStar/LoopNet data business is a subscription utility that brokers, lenders, REITs, and appraisers can't operate without. That base cash-flow engine is what funded the residential build without diluting shareholders or levering up.

3. **CEO conviction buy at the lows.** Andy Florance — founder/CEO since 1986 — bought **83,300 shares for ~$2.49M at $29.89 on Aug 4, 2026**, near multi-year lows. Insiders were net buyers of 462k shares over the trailing 6 months. Founder open-market buys of this size, this close to the bottom, are among the higher-signal insider events.

4. **Valuation is at a 7-year low on forward numbers.** Forward P/E ~19x and forward EV/2026E EBITDA of ~16.5x for a 15-18% grower with 78% gross margins is a large discount to CoStar's own history and to lower-quality peers (Zillow frequently trades at multiples well above this). The trailing P/E of 181x is optically scary but is a rear-view artifact of the residential-investment trough — the forward math is what matters at an inflection.

5. **Balance sheet gives them the whole runway.** ~$1.27B cash vs ~$1.16B debt (near net-cash), plus a $700M buyback authorization for 2026. No financing risk, no forced timeline — management can let the operating leverage play out on its own schedule.

## Why this could fail

1. **Bookings momentum is deteriorating even as margins improve.** Net new bookings were $69M in Q2, **down 26% YoY**, pressured by a Ten-X restructuring, Homes.com efficiency cuts, and aggressive competitor pricing in multifamily/Apartments.com. Bookings are the forward revenue tell — margins can inflect while the top-line growth engine quietly cools. This is the most important yellow flag in the story.

2. **Revenue guide was cut, not raised.** FY26 revenue was revised **down** to $3.715-3.755B (~+15%) on sales-force optimization, Ten-X restructuring, and "price integrity" moves in Apartments.com. The EBITDA improvement is partly a margin/cost story, not pure demand — a lower-quality kind of beat.

3. **Homes.com full profitability is years out.** Q2 was the first positive-EBITDA residential quarter, but management still targets **run-rate profitability only by 2029 and full-year by 2030**. Residential remains a structural drag and a Zillow slugfest for years; one bad quarter re-opens the burn narrative.

4. **Valuation still requires the story to work.** At ~16.5x forward EBITDA the stock is cheap versus its history but not cheap in absolute terms. If margin expansion stalls or bookings keep sliding, there's real downside — a -64% drawdown proves the market will punish this name hard.

5. **Trend damage is real.** "Basing" is a hopeful label on a chart that fell from $92 to $26. A break of the recent base ($26 low) invalidates the technical setup regardless of fundamentals.

## Probability-weighted EV

Anchoring on FY26 EBITDA ~$800M and normalizing forward. Rough scenario framing on the ~$32.6 base:

| Scenario | Prob | What happens | Fair value | Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | 30% | Q3/Q4 confirm CRE margin expansion + residential EBITDA scaling; bookings stabilize; re-rate toward 22-24x fwd EBITDA | ~$50 | +53% |
| Base | 45% | Margins grind higher, residential stays modestly positive, bookings soft but not falling apart; partial re-rate | ~$40 | +23% |
| Bear | 25% | Bookings keep sliding, multifamily price war worsens, residential dips back to breakeven; multiple compresses | ~$24 | -26% |

Weighted expected return ≈ (0.30 × 53%) + (0.45 × 23%) + (0.25 × -26%) ≈ **+16%**, with the bull case carrying an attractive convex payoff if the inflection is real. Skew is favorable because the balance sheet removes the tail-risk of forced financing.

## Position sizing

A **medium starter, ~2-3% of a speculative book.** Quality of the underlying business justifies more than a lottery ticket, but the bookings deceleration and years-long residential drag argue against a full-size position before Q3 confirms the trend. Scale to ~4-5% only on a clean Q3 print (CRE margin up, residential EBITDA growing, bookings flat-to-up).

## Entry plan

- **Starter now** at market (~$32-33); the CEO bought at $29.89 three weeks ago, so current levels are within a reasonable band of insider cost.
- **Add zone $28-30** if the market offers a pullback toward the CEO's basis — that's the highest-conviction add.
- **Confirmation add** after Q3 (Oct 27) if margins/bookings validate the thesis, even if it means paying up into the mid-$30s.

## Exit plan

- **Trim / take profits** into $48-52 (approach to bull-case fair value / ~22-24x fwd EBITDA).
- **Behavioral success trigger:** two consecutive quarters of expanding consolidated EBITDA margin *with* bookings flat-to-up — let winners run past the first target.
- **Stop / thesis-break on the downside:** close below **$25.89** (the 52-wk low) on a weekly basis, OR a quarter where residential swings back to negative EBITDA and bookings fall another double-digit percent YoY. Either breaks the inflection thesis — take the loss, don't average into a re-breakdown.

## Hedges (for larger positions)

Options are liquid (OI ~2,134 calls / 3,843 puts). For a position sized above ~3%, finance downside with a modest put spread around earnings (e.g. Nov $30/$25) or express the whole view as Jan 2027 calls to cap risk at premium while keeping the convex upside.

## Watch items / hard dates

- **Q3 2026 earnings — 2026-10-27** (the key catalyst): CRE EBITDA margin trend, residential EBITDA (does +$12M scale?), and above all **net new bookings** — the number to fixate on.
- Apartments.com / multifamily competitive pricing commentary (the source of the bookings pressure).
- Ten-X restructuring progress and Homes.com agent-subscriber count (36k → ?).
- Any further Florance / insider open-market buys (or a stop) as a conviction tell.
- Homes.com traffic trend vs Zillow (was +119% organic YoY in Q1).

## Call

A genuine quality-compounder-in-a-margin-trough with a founder-CEO buying the lows and the bear thesis (residential burn) actively breaking — worth a medium starter now, but keep the bookings deceleration front and center and let Q3 decide whether to size up. **Conviction 6/10.**
