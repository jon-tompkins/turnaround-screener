*Communication Services / Telecom Services*

**Setup:** Charter has been crushed (from ~$286 to ~$149, a fresh 52-week low) as broadband subscriber losses accelerated to ~172K in a quarter under fixed-wireless and fiber assault. The bull case rests on the $34.5B Cox merger closing and a huge, cheap free-cash-flow base.

**Catalyst:** Cox merger close plus a quarter showing broadband net-adds stabilizing (losses narrowing) (Merger close expected summer/H2 2026; Q3 earnings ~2026-10-30)

**Suggested trade:** Small starter spot only near the 52-week low; scale only if a quarter shows broadband losses decelerating. Not a size-up until the sub bleed slows.

**Conviction:** 5/10 | Upside est: 50% | Downside est: 30%

**Bullish:**
- Extremely cheap on cash flow: fwd P/E ~3.4, EV/EBITDA ~5.4, ~$2.2B FCF
- $34.5B Cox acquisition expected to close, adding ~6.5M customers and ~$800M operating synergies
- 12M+ mobile lines is a genuine growth vector cross-selling into the broadband base
- Management expects Cox scale plus network upgrades to improve internet unit trajectory
- Street mean target (~$184) implies meaningful upside from a washed-out price

**Bearish:**
- Core broadband is in secular decline -- 172K sub losses and management warns competition stays 'fierce'
- Balance sheet is heavy: debt/equity ~441, current ratio 0.36; the Cox deal adds more leverage
- Cord-cutting continues to erode legacy video
- Fixed wireless (T-Mobile/Verizon) and fiber overbuilders are structural, not cyclical, headwinds
- 'Cheap on FCF' is the classic value-trap profile if the FCF base itself is eroding

**Key risk:** Broadband subscriber losses keep accelerating, making the low multiple a melting-ice-cube rather than a bargain.
